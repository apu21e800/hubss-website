# From job photos to a Projects article, by itself

For Doug. One page. Since 19 October 2026 a finished job needs one thing from
you: its photos and a few lines about it, in Studio. The rest happens on its
own: an article on hubss.com under **Projects**, and the social posts for it.

## What you do (five minutes, on your phone)

1. Studio (**hubss.com/studio**) → **Job photos for social** → **+**.
2. Add 3 to 10 photos, best one first (it leads the article and the social
   posts), alt text on each: what is in the picture, in a line.
3. **Where**: town and province, spelled out. Add the site if the client is
   happy to be named.
4. **HUB system**, and the **Application** if you like.
5. **What was done**: one or two sentences of facts.
6. **The story**: this is the new part. A few sentences or a list, in your
   own words: who the client was (if they can be named), what they needed,
   what went in and when, who installed it, anything about the design or the
   colours, and how it has held up. Everything the article says about the job
   comes from here and from the line above, so no guesses and no figures you
   aren't sure of. Leave it empty and the photos become social posts only, as
   before.
7. **Publish**.

## What happens next

- **The next morning (10:00 Toronto time, 9:00 after the clocks go back)** the
  article is written from your words and the system's approved copy, and
  nothing else. It gets the house style pass and the fact check every draft
  gets. It is saved in **Blog posts** with **Goes live by itself** switched on,
  for the morning two weekdays later (a Monday post goes Wednesday; a Thursday
  post goes the next Monday; two jobs posted the same day go out on two days).
  The first photo is the featured photo; the others close the article under
  "The job in photographs", with your captions.
- **You get an email** with the title, the opening, the fact check and the
  link. Read it when you have a minute:
  - **To change a line**, edit it in Studio. It still goes out on time.
  - **To hold it**, open it and switch **Goes live by itself** off. It then
    waits for you, like any draft.
  - **To move it**, change **Goes live at** to a later day.
  - **To let it go**, do nothing.
- **On the morning**, it goes live. You get a second email with the address;
  the page is there about five minutes later, once the site has rebuilt.
- **The morning after that**, its social posts go to Buffer, one per channel,
  with the photo and a tracked link. Whether they wait there for your
  approval or go out by themselves at the next slot is a setting Vern holds
  (docs/buffer-setup.md); the email says which.

A job post with a story makes no separate social post of its own: the job goes
out once, as the article, with a link to the full write-up.

## Things worth knowing

- The article never says anything about the job that your words don't. If
  the fact check finds a line it can't match, or an em dash the style pass
  couldn't fix, the draft is **held**: it waits in Blog posts with "Goes
  live by itself" off, the email's subject says "Needs a read before it
  goes", and the draft's **Notes for the editor** quote the line. Fix it,
  then switch "Goes live by itself" on or press Publish. Nothing unchecked
  goes on the site by itself.
- The email's "Goes live" day is a weekday morning. Nothing goes out on a
  weekend.
- A live article is edited like any other: change it, press **Publish**, and
  the site updates within seconds.
- The job post itself shows **The article written from this** once the
  article exists, so you can get from the photos to the article in one click.
- Any blog draft can be scheduled the same way: switch **Goes live by
  itself** on and pick the time.
