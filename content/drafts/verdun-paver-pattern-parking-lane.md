What this parking lane in Verdun, Québec, takes is listed in HUB Surface Systems' Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5: "turning tires, delivery trucks and plow blades." The lane is [TrafficPatternsXD](/products/traffic-patterns-xd), laid along the curb to look like grey pavers, and the book says it takes all of that "without the shifting and shoving of real pavers."

## Along the curb in the photograph

The photograph on [page 131](/product-book/131) is taken from the lane itself, low enough to show the speckle of aggregate in every grey brick. The bricks run in offset rows down the curb as far as the parked cars, with a paler border on the traffic side, and a delivery truck is in the travel lane beside them.

## One sheet with no units to shift

Each sheet of TrafficPatternsXD is 150 mil thick, with its 8–9 Mohs aggregate running through the full cross-section, and asphalt is the only surface it goes on. The sheet is heated on the prepared asphalt and stamped, which presses the brick pattern through the material and into the top of the asphalt, so the bricks are one sheet, not separate units. The page's headline is about how long a parking lane like this lasts, and the system page, [page 13](/product-book/13), puts a figure on it: a service life of 10+ years, with a skid resistance of 60 BPN (ASTM E303).

What pavers do on a plow route, and what a surface without units does instead, is the subject of [the guide to snowplow-safe decorative pavement](/blog/snowplow-safe-decorative-pavement).

## A lane that narrows the road

The book gives the pattern a second job: "The pattern also defines the lane visually, narrowing the roadway and calming roadway traffic." The [traffic calming](/applications/traffic-calming) spread, [page 54](/product-book/54), opens with "Drivers slow down with visual cues," then names TrafficPatternsXD for "durable road surface treatments."
