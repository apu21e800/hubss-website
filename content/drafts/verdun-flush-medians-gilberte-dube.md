The road narrows at the corner of Rue Gilberte-Dubé and Rue Jacques-Lauzon, on Île-des-Sœurs in Verdun, Québec, where a flush median of [TrafficPatternsXD](/products/traffic-patterns-xd) curves out from the curb. HUB Surface Systems prints the corner on [page 116](/product-book/116) of the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5, labelled "Traffic control", and the page's text opens with what the system is doing there: "Utilizing TrafficPatternsXD to create visual and tactile traffic control methods."

## Charcoal bricks at the corner

The street sign is in the wider frame of the photograph above: Rue Gilberte-Dubé, with a green sign under it that reads Jeu libre (free play) and shows a figure kicking a ball. The median's field is charcoal bricks in offset rows, ringed by a border of pale bricks set crosswise, on dark asphalt. Brick houses face the street, and cars are parked along the far side.

## A median drivers see and feel

The book's next sentence gives the effect and the method together: "Slowing down cars, protecting pedestrians all while using the existing road surface with this snowplow-proof, road-narrowing installation." The road surface here is asphalt, the only one TrafficPatternsXD goes on. Its 150 mil sheets are aggregate-reinforced, heated on the prepared asphalt and stamped into it, and the system page, [page 13](/product-book/13), credits the material with high colour contrast for increased safety and a service life of 10+ years.

## Medians on the book's traffic calming list

The book's Traffic calming spread, on [page 54](/product-book/54), lists medians among the devices it covers, with roundabouts, truck aprons, gateway treatments and speed tables. HUB's [traffic calming page](/applications/traffic-calming) puts it from the driver's seat: "Traffic calming works when a driver sees the street change before a sign says so."
