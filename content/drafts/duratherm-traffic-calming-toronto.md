A white triangle points up a tree-lined street in Toronto, Ontario, its field broken into rows of dark rectangles. It is a traffic calming marking in [DuraTherm](/products/duratherm), the inlaid thermoplastic in the HUB Surface Systems range, and the photograph takes all of page 34 in [the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5](/product-book/34), facing the system's own page.

The photograph puts the marking on a stretch of darker asphalt, with the paler road running on between lawns and tall trees, past houses, to a white van in the distance.

## White thermoplastic inlaid in a stamped surface

DuraTherm starts with the road. The asphalt is heated and stamped first, then precut 90 mil thermoplastic (about 2.3 mm) is set into the impressions and bonded with an infrared heater. In this marking, then, the white is the thermoplastic, and the dark rectangles within it are the asphalt itself, the same surface as the road around them.

Nothing stands above the road. Page 35 says so in its own words: “nothing for a plow blade to catch, nothing to trip on.” Under Snowplow safe, it prints “no shear risk”.

## Traffic calming that keeps clear of the plow

The site files the job under [traffic calming](/applications/traffic-calming). The book's traffic calming spread starts from the driver: “A change in surface is a change in speed.” Its body says how: “Changing colour and texture tells a driver the environment has changed before any sign does.”

The site's DuraTherm page names speed tables beside crosswalks, calming and identity work, and it gives the case for inlaying a marking at all: the system is specified “where winter maintenance would strip anything that sits on top”.

The plow is also the subject of [the guide to plow-safe decorative pavement](/blog/snowplow-safe-decorative-pavement), which has a paragraph on DuraTherm.
