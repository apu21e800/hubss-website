The [Children's Safety Village of Brant](https://brantsafetyvillage.ca/), at 407 Elgin Street in Brantford, Ontario, is a miniature town where children aged 4 to 12 learn the basics of safety from local police and fire officers. Its streets have a working railway crossing, with a gate arm, signal lights and a signal case marked “Donated by CN”.

The sidewalk beside that crossing now carries a DecoMark marking of the Look Listen Live symbol: the yellow diamond with a locomotive and the campaign's address, looklistenlive.ca.

## The message on the sidewalk

Look Listen Live is [Operation Lifesaver Canada's rail safety campaign](https://looklistenlive.ca/). Its videos show how quiet and fast an oncoming train can be, and how a train can look slower and farther away than it is. The campaign's line is “Stay back. Stay safe. Stay alive.”

Operation Lifesaver puts the stakes plainly: “Virtually every rail-related incident that leaves someone dead or injured could have been avoided.”

## Why it works at a children's crossing

On the kindergarten visit, children practise how to stop, look and listen before they cross the village's roads. A marking on the sidewalk puts the same reminder where a child is already looking: at the ground, a few steps before the crossing.

## What DecoMark is

[DecoMark](/products/decomark) is HUB's custom graphics system: preformed thermoplastic, fabricated in the factory from vector artwork, then heat-applied to asphalt or concrete, where it bonds to the surface. Here it went onto the concrete sidewalk. It's slip-resistant underfoot, and it holds its contrast through freeze-thaw and plow seasons.

The same system makes community crests, wayfinding and recognition markers, as well as rail safety reminders like this one.

## Bringing it to your community

A rail crossing near a school or a trail is a good candidate, and so is any safety message a municipality wants people to read at their feet. Because DecoMark is made from vector artwork, a campaign symbol works as well as a crest or a town's own message.
