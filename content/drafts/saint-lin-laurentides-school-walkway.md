Long bands of orange and sand run the length of a walkway to the glass doors of a new primary school in Saint-Lin-Laurentides, Québec. The colour comes from [StreetBond](/products/streetbond), one of HUB Surface Systems' pavement coatings, laid in stripes that follow the line of the walk.

## Stripes that lead to the school doors

In the photograph the stripes start in the foreground and stop at the entrance: four bands across the walk, orange and sand in turn, with dark asphalt alongside. Young trees stand in planting beds edged with rows of wooden posts, and a play structure sits behind the posts on the left. The building is clad in white and pale yellow panels. The scene fills the whole of [page 120](/product-book/120) in the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5.

## What StreetBond does on a walk

StreetBond's spec line in the book reads "Water-based, epoxy-modified acrylic", and it goes on asphalt or concrete. Its page, [page 25](/product-book/25), opens its text with "Designed to move with the pavement," and among the failures of rigid coatings that this avoids it lists "being slippery in wet conditions." The book's figure for its service life is 8+ years, and the note beside that figure is "easily refreshed."

## Colour on a path with one destination

HUB's [parks and paths](/applications/parks-paths) page says StreetBond puts UV-stable colour on paths and plazas so that a route can double as wayfinding. This route has one end, and the stripes run straight at it.
