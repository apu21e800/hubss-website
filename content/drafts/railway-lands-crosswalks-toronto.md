These crosswalks are on downtown streets in Toronto, Ontario, and they are HUB Surface Systems' [TrafficPatternsXD](/products/traffic-patterns-xd), a preformed thermoplastic with aggregate mixed through it. [Page 91 of the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5](/product-book/91) prints them under the headline “Toronto's city streets.” and says that “this durable surface continues to perform 11 years later”.

The book's photograph catches a pedestrian in red running shoes mid-stride on the crosswalk, a grid of square units in alternating bands of tan and charcoal. Behind, towers with balconies line the street and people in business clothes walk along the sidewalk.

## 11 years of non-stop use

The page says where the system was put and what it has taken since. TrafficPatternsXD crosswalks are “used where maintenance-free surfaces are needed”, and these have had “non-stop use in the toughest urban environments”. The book gives TrafficPatternsXD a service life of 10+ years.

The book's crosswalks spread states their purpose: “Providing increased visibility and safety for pedestrians.” The site's [crosswalks](/applications/crosswalks) page sets TrafficPatternsXD beside the other systems a crosswalk can be built in.

## Tan and charcoal squares stamped into the asphalt

The grid in the photograph is made in the material itself. TrafficPatternsXD panels, 150 mil thick (about 3.8 mm), are heated on the asphalt, and a template pressed through them into the asphalt's top layer forms the joints between the squares.

Page 13, the system's own, is headed “For the toughest environments.” The aggregate mixed through the material is hard, 8–9 on the Mohs scale by the book's figure. The page also says the system is “proven in high traffic with concentrated wheel loads” and rates its skid resistance at 60 BPN (ASTM E303).
