A pothole has opened in the drive aisle by the front entrance, and it is January. There is a bag of cold patch asphalt in the maintenance shed, and the paving contractor says hot mix will have to wait for spring. Those are the usual two answers, and there is a third.

## What cold patch asphalt is for

The City of Toronto's pothole page says it plainly. In the winter months, when temperatures are low and the pavement is covered in snow or ice, "it is extremely difficult to work with hot asphalt because it cools very quickly," so "potholes are temporarily patched with cold mix asphalt to make the road safe" ([City of Toronto, Potholes](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/streets-parking-transportation/road-maintenance/potholes/)).

Greater Sudbury's page gives the other side. Cold mix "can be applied directly under all weather conditions, but is susceptible to road bed movement, vehicle traffic and snow removal operations" ([City of Greater Sudbury, Potholes](https://www.greatersudbury.ca/live/transportation-parking-and-roads/road-maintenance/potholes/)).

That is the job cold patch asphalt does well: it fills the hole tonight, by hand, in whatever weather there is, and makes the lane safe to drive. The repair is still to come. On a lot, that means the patch goes on the spring list the day it goes in, and somebody looks at it after the plows have been through.

## Hot mix, when the plants are open

Greater Sudbury calls hot mix patching "the more cost effective form of pothole repair," then names the catch: "during the winter, local asphalt plants shut down." The same page says when the holes come: "pothole issues are most common in the late winter and early spring." Toronto's page puts it in its own words: "More permanent repairs are performed with hot asphalt when warmer conditions prevail."

Hot mix is asphalt made hot at a plant, then placed and compacted by a paving crew while it is still hot. When the plant is open and a crew can come, it is the repair both cities make. The trouble is the calendar. The holes come in late winter and early spring, the plants open when it is warmer, and until then each hole waits under its cold patch.

## A repair that goes in year-round

[ChipFill](/products/chipfill)'s product page gives its weather as "Any, year-round" and its return to service as minutes, and says it needs "no specialized equipment and no hot-mix plant." It is one of HUB's three repair products, and all three go in year-round.

ChipFill is thermoplastic in small chips, poured into the prepared hole, levelled and heated with a propane torch until it bonds to the asphalt or concrete around it. Its page names smaller potholes, cracks and joints.

For a hole too deep or wide for ChipFill alone, [AggreFill](/products/aggrefill) fills the void: a pre-coated aggregate, poured cold and finished with the torch and ChipFill, which bonds the aggregate and seals the repair flush. Its page names parking lot craters and deep road failures among its uses, and the lane reopens within minutes.

[Fast Patch DPR](/products/fast-patch), the repair in the photo at the top of this page, is a pourable polymer blend for potholes, spalls, joints, wheel paths and utility cuts in asphalt and concrete. Its page gives the return to service as about an hour at 21°C, and the optional Fast Patch Kicker accelerator shortens the cure in cooler conditions. Because it is odourless and 100% solids, it also goes indoors, on warehouse floors.

[The crack filler guide](/blog/asphalt-crack-filler-cracks-joints-potholes) walks a lot with all three and matches each to the crack, the open joint and the pothole.

## When the next one opens

- To make it safe tonight with what is in the shed: cold patch asphalt, and the repair on the list the same day.
- To repair it when the plant is open and a crew can come: hot mix.
- To repair it now, in any month: ChipFill for a small pothole, AggreFill finished with ChipFill for a deep or wide one, and Fast Patch DPR for spalls, wheel paths and utility cuts, outdoors or in.
