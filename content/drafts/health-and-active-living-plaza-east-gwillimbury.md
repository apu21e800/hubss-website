In the photograph the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 prints on page 125, a family crosses a blue and pale grey crosswalk in East Gwillimbury, Ontario, a small child riding a blue toy car beside two adults. Behind them a pylon names the Health and Active Living Plaza.

The crosswalk is HUB Surface Systems' [TrafficPatternsXD](/products/traffic-patterns-xd), a 150 mil preformed thermoplastic, and the page's headline is “Community centres.” Its one sentence says what the colour is for: “Using TrafficPatternsXD in a bold colour scheme highlights the location of the new community centre, using materials that will hold up to the wear and tear of a busy community location.”

## Blue and pale grey bands at the plaza

The bold colour scheme, in the photograph, is blue and pale grey in a brick pattern. Blue frames the crosswalk and bands of blue and pale grey alternate across it, with a strip of pale grey along each outer edge, against the dark asphalt of the road.

That contrast is one of the strengths the book's page 13 claims for the system: “High colour contrast for increased safety.” The system's colours are the thermoplastic range on pages 14 and 15, printed under the line “Formulated for UV stability.”

## A crosswalk on the parks and paths pages

The book files the page under Parks & Paths, the site's [parks and paths](/applications/parks-paths) application. What the photograph shows is a road crosswalk beside the plaza's pylon, and the book's spread on [crosswalks](/applications/crosswalks) lists TrafficPatternsXD first, at “150 mil, aggregate-reinforced”, under the headline “Crosswalks that hold their colour and their grip.”

## Wear and tear at a busy location

TrafficPatternsXD is heated in place and stamped with a template that pushes the pattern through the panels and into the asphalt underneath; it goes on asphalt only. Its anti-skid aggregate is mixed through the whole thickness of the material, so the grip lasts as long as the colour, and the book puts its service life at 10+ years.
