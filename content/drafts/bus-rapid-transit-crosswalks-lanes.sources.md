## How a bus rapid transit corridor wears

- "concentrate wear into a few square metres": book p.52, the transit corridors spread, word for word (lib/idea-book-print.ts:235). "The transit corridor page": the edition's eyebrow for that spread is "Transit corridors" (lib/idea-book-edition.ts:239). First mention of the book in full, "Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5".
- "At a crossing the wear comes from buses braking and turning on the same patch of road": london-east-link-brt ("buses brake, turn and pull in at the same spot, all day, and the wear concentrates into a few square metres").
- "a crosswalk under that traffic wears out by losing its contrast and its grip": london-east-link-brt ("Crosswalks fail differently: they lose contrast and they lose grip").
- "the loads gather at the stop pads, the turning movements and the stations": book p.93 (lib/idea-book-edition.ts:319: "Bus lanes fail where the loads concentrate: stop pads, turning movements, stations").
- "one continuous field that a driver reads, with no sign needed, as a lane for the buses": london-east-link-brt ("a continuous colour field that tells a driver, without a sign, that this space is not theirs").
- "A lane also has to go in without shutting the corridor down": london-east-link-brt ("they have to go in without shutting the corridor down"). "the closure a road can give counts as much as the material": the same post ("In an active transit corridor that number [the cure time] is the specification").
