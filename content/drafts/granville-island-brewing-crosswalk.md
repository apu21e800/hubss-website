On Granville Island in Vancouver, British Columbia, the street in front of Granville Island Brewing has a crosswalk in a red brick pattern, and it ends at the sidewalk outside the taproom door. The crosswalk is [TrafficPatternsXD](/products/traffic-patterns-xd), and HUB Surface Systems prints it in the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 under the headline "The first thing a customer walks on."

## Seven years old in the photograph

The book's photograph looks along the crosswalk to the brewery: a red brick pattern in running bond between two white edge lines, with the "Granville Island Brewing" sign high on the wall above the taproom's glass canopy. The cab and front wheels of a concrete truck fill the left of the frame, beside the crosswalk. The book labels the photograph on [page 130](/product-book/130) "7 years old," in white letters in its corner.

## How TrafficPatternsXD goes in

The book introduces TrafficPatternsXD as "The toughest material in the product range": aggregate-reinforced preformed thermoplastic, 150 mil thick. The sheets are heated where they lie on the prepared asphalt; then a template, vibrated into the soft material, carries the brick pattern down into the top of the asphalt, which is the only surface the system goes on. The aggregate is rated 8–9 on the Mohs scale and runs through the full thickness, and its skid resistance is 60 BPN under ASTM E303.

For service life the book gives 10+ years, and its description of the material includes "Proven in high traffic with concentrated wheel loads."
