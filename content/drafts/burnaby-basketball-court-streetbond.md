Burnaby, British Columbia, has a basketball court with a flower at centre court: orange petals outlined in white on a green ground, coated in [StreetBond](/products/streetbond). HUB Surface Systems prints it across a full page of the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5, [page 118](/product-book/118), with no headline and no text: only the photograph, and a caption naming the city and the coating.

## Petals around a white tree

The photograph shows the petals spreading out from a blue star at the middle of the court, where a white post stands. The post branches like a tree, and the backboards are mounted on its branches. Beyond the court are young trees, lamp posts, a row of parked cars and high-rise towers on the skyline.

## A hard place for colour

On its [sport courts](/applications/sport-courts) spread, the book says: “A court is one of the hardest colour environments in outdoor pavement.” It specifies StreetBond for “court colour on asphalt or concrete”, and calls it UV-stable.

The coating moves as the asphalt or concrete beneath it moves, and the book credits that with avoiding what goes wrong with rigid coatings: cracking, premature wear and slipperiness when wet. Worn lines on a court are recoated; the book calls StreetBond easily refreshed and puts its service life at 8+ years.
