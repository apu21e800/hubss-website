A parking bay at the front door of a commercial building is where a visit starts: the car stops on it, and the customer steps out onto it. At a new commercial building in Mission, British Columbia, the bays out front are herringbone [StreetPrint stamped asphalt](/products/streetprint), coloured in [StreetBond](/products/streetbond) Pewter and installed in 2025.

## Herringbone up to the drive aisle

The job has page 137 of the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 to itself, under the headline "Commercial hardscapes." In the book's photograph the grey herringbone fills each bay from the concrete wheel stops at the walk to the plain black asphalt of the drive aisle. Thin white lines divide the bays, and a white border runs along the outside edge of the nearest one. The building's glass front and its brick and metal siding stand along one side, with mountains on the skyline.

The page is written about frontages of this kind: "Retail entries, hotel forecourts, and mixed-use frontages where the surface underfoot signals the quality of everything inside." Its caption reads "Mission, British Columbia | StreetPrint", and the whole page is in [the book online at page 137](/product-book/137).

## Stamped into the asphalt and coloured Pewter

With StreetPrint, a pattern is pressed into heated asphalt, new or existing, and then coloured with StreetBond pavement coating. The pattern is in the asphalt itself, so, as the book's StreetPrint page puts it, "The surface stays flush." On that page StreetPrint's service life is 10–20 years and its patterns come standard or custom; Herringbone and Diagonal Herringbone both appear on [the book's list of field templates](/product-book/23).

StreetBond, the colour coat, is a water-based, epoxy-modified acrylic made for asphalt and concrete, and the book puts its own service life at 8+ years, easily refreshed. Pewter is one of the greys in the StreetBond range printed on page 27 of the book.

## Where StreetPrint fits on a commercial site

HUB Surface Systems uses StreetPrint at the entrances and pedestrian routes of a [parking lot](/applications/parking-lots), for the look of pavers with nothing to settle or weed. Page 78, the book's parking lots page, lists StreetPrint with its StreetBond coat beside [DuraShield](/products/durashield), StreetBond and [TrafficPatternsXD](/products/traffic-patterns-xd). On HUB's page for [commercial spaces](/applications/commercial-spaces), the same system takes the entry courts and walkways, while TrafficPatternsXD carries the crosswalks from the lot to the doors.
