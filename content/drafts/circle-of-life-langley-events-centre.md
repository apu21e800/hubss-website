A grandstand and a tall light standard rise behind the plaza at the Langley Events Centre in Langley, British Columbia, and on the plaza lies a wide circle of colour. It is Circle of Life, a piece of public art made in [StreetBond](/products/streetbond), the HUB Surface Systems coating that puts colour on asphalt and concrete.

## Forms on a sand-coloured ground

The circle's ground is the colour of sand. A long orange form runs nearly the width of it, under a cluster of blue shapes at the top; a deep blue crescent follows the lower edge, green leaf shapes reach into it on one side, and teal forms fill the other. Each colour meets the sand at a crisp edge. To the left of the plaza stand a covered pavilion and a flagpole, and a row of white pickup trucks waits at its far edge.

## Large fields of colour

The Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 heads its public art spread, on [page 66](/product-book/66), "Public art, at the scale of the street." Its note for StreetBond reads "large-scale colour fields", and Circle of Life is made of little else. The same spread gives artwork and graphics to [DecoMark](/products/decomark) and large interconnected sheets to [TrafficPatterns](/products/traffic-patterns), so a piece of this kind, broad shapes of flat colour, is StreetBond's work.

On asphalt or concrete the coating flexes with the pavement, and the book counts that flexibility as what spares it the cracking and early wear of rigid coatings. Its service life, as the book has it, is 8+ years, and the [public art](/applications/public-art) page draws the practical conclusion: an installed piece lasts as long as the system it is made from.

Colours for a piece like this start from the 63 standard StreetBond colours on [pages 26 and 27 of the book](/product-book/26), and the book offers custom Pantone matching for anything outside them, so the palette can be the artist's own.
