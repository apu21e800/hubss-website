## Read this first: four things only Doug can settle

They are in the note he reads in Studio (`forDoug`, printed as "To confirm before Publish: ..."):

1. **The system.** The Town's note corrects the Barrie 360 article: it "does say Decomark instead of TrafficPatterns which was a bit of a mistake". So the post says TrafficPatterns. The installation photos show a wheeled infrared heater on a frame (lettered StreetHeat SR-120) over the pieces. If Doug says TrafficPatternsXD, three things change: the product link and name in the first paragraph and the excerpt, the "Heat-applied on the road" section (XD is heated and stamped into the asphalt, 150 mil, 10+ years, page 13), and `relatedProducts`.
2. **Who installed it.** The notes say HUB helped "in getting this installed" and with "teaching us about the product"; they do not say whose crew did the work. The post names nobody.
3. **Naming and quoting the Town's project administrator.** The post says "the Town's operations staff wrote to HUB that the crosswalk was received very well". His words were "It was received very well ... we received compliments from the Legion and the Mayor and Council on the crosswalk." If he agrees to be quoted, that sentence becomes a quotation with his name and title.
4. **The photos.** All four used are the Town's, sent for HUB's website at Doug's request, credited "Photo by the Town of Midland" in each caption. A fifth (the crew at work, two faces) is left out until someone says it can go in.

## First paragraph

- "Members of Royal Canadian Legion Branch 80 unveiled Midland's Veterans Memorial Crosswalk on November 6, 2026": the news post's caption, "Members of the Royal Canadian Legion Branch 80 officially unveiled Midland's Veterans Memorial Crosswalk on November 6, 2026." The Town's first note: "We had an unveiling event on the 6th".
- "five days before Remembrance Day": November 6 to November 11.
- "It crosses at King Street and Ellen Street in Midland, Ontario, beside the Midland Cenotaph": the news post, "at the intersection of King Street and Ellen Street, adjacent to the Midland Cenotaph"; the cenotaph is in the park behind the crosswalk in two of the photos.
- "where the Legion holds its Remembrance Day ceremony": the news post, "Royal Canadian Legion Branch 80 will host its Remembrance Day ceremony at the Midland Cenotaph".
- "The crosswalk is TrafficPatterns preformed thermoplastic from HUB Surface Systems.": the Town's second note (above). To confirm with Doug (1).

## Maple leaves and one line in two languages

- "The Legion provided the design, the Town says.": the news post, "Featuring a design provided by the Legion".
- "Red maple leaves run down the middle of the crosswalk, over bars of grey and white inside a white border.": the photos (all four of the finished crosswalk).
- The English line, "In honour of those who served and sacrificed.": read off the photo of that end; the news post's image description gives the same words.
- The French line, "En hommage à ceux et celles qui ont servi et se sont sacrifiés.": read off the two photos of the other end. A fallen leaf lies on the last word in both; the word is "sacrifiés".
- "Seen from the French end, the cenotaph stands in the park behind the crosswalk": the cover photo.

## Heat-applied on the road

- "The artwork goes into the material at the factory. TrafficPatterns is preformed thermoplastic made to the design, 125 mil thick, and on site the finished pieces are heat-applied to the road, where they bond to its surface.": lib/products.ts:119 ("factory-made to your design at 125 mil and heat-applied to asphalt or concrete, where it bonds to the surface"); page 11, Thickness 125 mil. The same two sentences are in the Every Child Matters write-up.
- "The Town's photos of the installation show a heater passing over a maple leaf, with the grey and white bars already laid around it.": the two installation photos used.
- "The anti-skid grit is mixed right through the material, so the crosswalk keeps its grip as it wears.": page 11 ("Anti-skid aggregate is intermixed through the full cross-section, so the grip lasts as long as the colour."); docs/GLOSSARY.md, "anti-skid aggregate".
- "a skid resistance of 60 BPN, measured to ASTM E303, and a service life of 8+ years": page 11 (lib/idea-book-print.ts:71, :73); link /product-book/11.

## How it was received

- "After the unveiling, the Town's operations staff wrote to HUB that the crosswalk was received very well, with compliments from the Legion, the Mayor and Council.": the Town's first note, from its project administrator for operations. Paraphrased, nobody named. To confirm with Doug (3).
- "HUB had worked with the Town's staff on the product before it went down.": the same note thanks Doug for "the part you played in getting this installed and helping us out with pricing as well as teaching us about the product". The post leaves the pricing out.
- "The Town's own account of the day, with a photograph of pipers and veterans crossing": the news post and its image description ("A group of bagpipers and veterans crosses a commemorative crosswalk"). Linked.
- "A memorial crosswalk is still a crosswalk. It meets the same plows and the same salt as the one at the next corner, so it is specified the same way, by its grip and its service life.": the writer's point, from the system's own line (lib/products.ts:119: "engineered for snowplow blades, de-icing salts") and page 11's two figures above.
- "Kitchener's veterans crosswalk, also in TrafficPatterns": the published post /blog/veterans-crosswalk-kitchener ("Kitchener's veterans crosswalk in TrafficPatterns").
