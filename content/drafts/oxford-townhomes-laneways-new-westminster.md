Residents of Oxford Townhomes, a strata development in Queensborough, New Westminster, British Columbia, have garages that open onto its laneways, and the lanes themselves are charcoal [StreetPrint stamped asphalt](/products/streetprint), coloured with [StreetBond](/products/streetbond). HUB Surface Systems' Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 begins its Projects section with a photograph of them, on [page 80](/product-book/80).

## A field of stone and a band along the curb

The laneway is wet in the photograph. Beyond a white line across it, the asphalt carries a field of stones of different sizes, and in the foreground the stamping narrows to a band of larger stones along the curb, beside plain asphalt. Grey three-storey townhomes with white garage doors stand on both sides, and a curbed planting bed takes the corner on the right.

## A pattern pressed into the lane

StreetPrint, a Canadian invention, has been installed since 1992, and the book calls it the original stamped asphalt. The pattern goes in with a steel wire-rope template pressed into heated asphalt, and then StreetBond coatings colour it, so the stones in the photograph are impressions in the lane itself. With no joints or pavers in it, the lane has nothing to weed and nothing to settle, and the finished surface stays flush. The book gives the system 10–20 years of service on new or existing asphalt. The process on site is set out in [the stamped asphalt install guide](/blog/how-stamped-asphalt-is-installed).

## Shared drives with the look of stone

The book's [townhomes](/applications/townhomes) spread is headed “Cohesive hardscape for strata developments.” It writes of entry courts and shared drives made to look like clay pavers or stone cobble, and its next sentence is “Almost no maintenance and easy to refresh.” On that spread StreetPrint does the stamping and StreetBond the colour and sealing.
