# Vision Zero Crosswalks: Why Canadian Municipalities Are Moving Beyond Traffic Paint

Canadian municipalities face a persistent challenge: traffic paint fades in 12–18 months, requires costly annual re-striping, and provides minimal retroreflectivity in wet conditions — the exact conditions when pedestrian visibility matters most.

Vision Zero commitments demand a better answer.

## The Problem with Painted Crosswalks

Standard thermoplastic paint applied at high-traffic intersections degrades rapidly under the combined stress of turning vehicles, winter freeze-thaw cycles, and aggressive snowplow operations. For municipalities with Vision Zero programs, this creates a dangerous gap: the crosswalk that was crisp and retroreflective at installation becomes a faded ghost marking within two seasons.

The data is unambiguous. A 2023 TAC study found that retroreflective performance of paint-based crosswalk markings dropped below acceptable thresholds in an average of 14 months on arterial roads — compared to 6+ years for preformed thermoplastic systems.

## Preformed Thermoplastic: The Municipal Standard

TrafficPatterns and TrafficPatternsXD preformed thermoplastic systems are factory-manufactured to precise mil-thickness specifications (90mil and 150mil respectively), then thermally bonded directly to the asphalt substrate. This monolithic bond — not an adhesive, not a coating — creates a marking that expands and contracts with the pavement through Canadian freeze-thaw cycles without cracking, lifting, or separating.

Key performance characteristics:

- **Retroreflectivity:** Exceeds TAC and MUTCD requirements for 5–7+ years
- **Skid resistance:** BPN 65+ maintained throughout service life
- **Snowplow resistance:** Zero documented edge damage in municipal installations
- **AODA compliance:** Trip-free, zero-elevation-change surface

## Vision Zero Applications Across Canada

York Region, the City of Toronto, Vancouver, and Richmond have all specified TrafficPatternsXD for their highest-priority pedestrian safety corridors. The Brighouse Station intersection in Richmond — a complex multi-modal crossing with high pedestrian and transit volumes — has maintained full retroreflective performance through three consecutive winters since installation.
