[Page 132 of the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5](/product-book/132) carries no headline. A photograph fills it, and the only words on the page are its caption, "Maple Ridge, British Columbia". The photograph is the roundabout at Kanaka Springs, and the red herringbone that rings its central island is [StreetPrint stamped asphalt](/products/streetprint) from HUB Surface Systems.

## Herringbone round the island

The ring is the roundabout's apron, the part a long truck's rear wheels track over while cars keep to the lane. Its herringbone runs as one straight grid from side to side, cut off by a concrete curb on the outside and by the island's curb on the inside, where a chevron sign points traffic around. The Kanaka Springs sign stands behind the roundabout, and in the picture cones are still out at the splitter islands, with construction fencing along the far side.

HUB's [traffic calming](/applications/traffic-calming) page says of StreetPrint that it "stamps a pattern into the asphalt of the calming device itself, so the texture is in the road rather than on it."

## A stamped pattern under a StreetBond coat

StreetPrint is two things at once, and the book's specification for it says so: "Stamping + StreetBond coating". The pattern is stamped into the asphalt with a template, and herringbone is one of the book's standard field templates, along with diagonal herringbone, offset brick and British cobble; custom templates are made on request. The colour is a coat of StreetBond laid over the stamped surface.

The two parts last for different spans. The book gives StreetPrint 10–20 years of service life and the StreetBond coat 8+ years, and [the guide to how stamped asphalt is installed](/blog/how-stamped-asphalt-is-installed) explains what that means for upkeep: the coating "runs its own cycle", and when the colour needs freshening, the surface gets a new coat.
