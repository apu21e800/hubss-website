A fleet lot is a wide field of pavement. The one in this photograph is in Toronto, Ontario, coated in [StreetBondSR](/products/streetbondsr), a solar reflective coating from HUB Surface Systems, and it is the picture on the system's own page in [the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5](/product-book/28).

Shot from low on the lot, the book's photograph shows the coated surface running to a long row of red trucks and white vans parked side by side along a low white building, with hydro towers beyond.

## StreetBond in colours that reflect the sun

StreetBondSR is the StreetBond epoxy-modified acrylic coating in colours the book describes as “formulated to reflect rather than absorb”. It goes on asphalt, and the book gives it a service life of 8+ years.

Solar reflectance is measured on a scale from 0 to 1. The SR colours carry 0.33 or higher when new, which the system's page prints as “≥ 0.33 initial”. The book's StreetBond colour pages set an SR figure under every swatch and name the tests, ASTM C1549 for reflectance and ASTM C1371 for emittance. Their advice is to “specify by name and request a physical sample for colour approval”.

## Parking first on the system's page

Parking leads the list of uses at the foot of the StreetBondSR page, and on the site this job sits with [parking lots](/applications/parking-lots).

The page's subhead is “Mitigating the urban heat island effect.” The urban heat island is the measurable difference in temperature between a city and the country around it, and dark pavement is one cause of it. The system's colours at SR 0.33 or higher can contribute to the LEED v5 Sustainable Sites credit for urban heat island reduction (non-roof), the subject of the site's page on [LEED and the urban heat island](/applications/leed-urban-heat-island).
