The walk to a school's doors should give nobody anything to trip on. At Immaculate Conception School in Vancouver, British Columbia, that walk is a honeycomb in two blues, [DuraTherm](/products/duratherm) inlaid in the asphalt, and its photograph fills [page 121](/product-book/121) of HUB Surface Systems' Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5.

## The way to the doors

In the book's photograph the honeycomb fills a wide band of asphalt from the foreground into the shade of a canopy on white posts, and stops at the school's double doors. On either side of it the yard is plain asphalt. The hexagons are the dark asphalt itself. The blue is in the lines between them: a deeper blue along both edges, and a pale blue that winds up the middle. The school's name is on the light blue wall beside the doors, next to an arc of blue stars.

The caption names the Catholic Diocese of Vancouver and the system. HUB's [public spaces](/applications/public-spaces) page says all of its systems go in over sound asphalt with no excavation, and the book's spread for public spaces carries the line "Dynamic surface treatments for high-visibility community projects."

## Inlaid so the walk stays flush

First the asphalt is heated and stamped with the honeycomb. The grout lines are the thermoplastic: precut at 90 mil (about 2.3 mm), they are set into the stamped impressions and bonded with an infrared heater. In the photograph, the two blues are that thermoplastic.

On [page 35](/product-book/35), the book describes the finished surface as "flush with the road," with "nothing to trip on." It lists DuraTherm as snowplow safe, with no shear risk. On streets, HUB puts the same system into crosswalks and speed tables, where a plow would strip anything that stood above the road.
