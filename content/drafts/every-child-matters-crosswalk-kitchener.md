A driver coming up to this crosswalk sees a band of orange across the road, edged in white. Someone on the sidewalk can read what is on it: the words "every child matters" in white script, and a white drawing of a shoe whose lace runs out into one long line around them. This Every Child Matters crosswalk in Ontario fills page 57 of HUB Surface Systems' Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5, opposite a page headed "Seen from the sidewalk as well as the driver's seat." It is [TrafficPatterns](/products/traffic-patterns) preformed thermoplastic.

## Line work that reads at two distances

The facing page is the book's creative design page, Application 06, and it calls this crosswalk Indigenous-designed, with "intricate line work that reads as art up close and as high-visibility contrast for drivers." For that kind of work the page specifies TrafficPatterns for custom designs and [DecoMark](/products/decomark) for custom graphics, both 125 mil preformed thermoplastic.

In the photograph, more white line work runs along the far end of the band, and a park in autumn colour lines one side of the road. A speed limit sign reading 40 stands on a post by the sidewalk; over the road hangs a sign with a walking figure.

## Made to the artwork in the factory

The artwork goes into the material at the factory. TrafficPatterns is preformed thermoplastic made to the design, 125 mil thick, and on site the finished pieces are heat-applied to the road, where they bond to its surface. The anti-skid grit is mixed right through the material, so the crosswalk keeps its grip as it wears. The book's TrafficPatterns page ([page 11](/product-book/11)) lists a skid resistance of 60 BPN, measured to ASTM E303; the service life it gives is 8+ years.

The spread, page 56 with this photograph opposite on page 57, is in [the Product Portfolio Book online](/product-book/56).
