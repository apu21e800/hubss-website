The crosswalk in front of Brampton Transit's Bramalea Terminal in Brampton, Ontario, is laid in bands of red and off-white squares between white edge lines, and it is [TrafficPatternsXD](/products/traffic-patterns-xd). In the photograph the terminal's canopy and glass front stand behind it, with buses lined up along the building and one of them lettered Brampton Transit. A pedestrian waits at the far corner.

## What the red and off-white bands are made of

The material is a 150 mil sheet of aggregate-reinforced preformed thermoplastic. Heated on prepared asphalt, the sheet is then stamped, and the stamp presses the square pattern through it into the top of the asphalt; TrafficPatternsXD is made for asphalt alone. On its own page the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 sets the system's service life at 10+ years and credits its high colour contrast with increased safety. The same page says it is "specified by Canadian transit authorities, municipalities and commercial stakeholders across Canada".

## The first spread in the book's applications

The book opens its applications with crosswalks, numbered Application 01, and this crosswalk is [the photograph on page 47](/product-book/47). The paragraph on the facing page says preformed thermoplastic holds its skid resistance and its "high-contrast colour through snowplow cycles and de-icing seasons". Under SPECIFY it puts TrafficPatternsXD first, at "150 mil, aggregate-reinforced", ahead of [TrafficPatterns](/products/traffic-patterns) at 125 mil.

The site's [crosswalks page](/applications/crosswalks) draws the line between the two. TrafficPatterns is heat-applied to asphalt or concrete and bonds to the surface, while the thicker TrafficPatternsXD is stamped into asphalt for intersections where traffic is heavy and turning.
