A pedestrian with a bag in each hand walks across a brick-pattern crosswalk at a parking lot entrance in Toronto, Ontario. The pattern is [TrafficPatternsXD](/products/traffic-patterns-xd), and in HUB Surface Systems' Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 the photograph has a page to itself, with nothing printed on it but the city and the system's name.

## A brick pattern from curb to curb

The photograph is close enough to pick out the joints between the bricks. They are a pale red, set in offset rows between two white edge lines, and they carry the walker from one curb across the drive toward the other. A silver SUV is at the far line, and behind it rows of parked cars fill the lot.

## The template that made the brick lines

TrafficPatternsXD goes down on asphalt only, in sheets of aggregate-reinforced preformed thermoplastic 150 mil thick. The lines in this crosswalk were made by a template vibrated "into the melted surface, while keying into the asphalt below," in the words of the book's [step pages](/product-book/16), once the panels were in place on the prepared asphalt and heated.

## Where the book puts it in a parking lot

On the Parking lots spread, [page 78](/product-book/78), the book's list of systems to specify names TrafficPatternsXD, 150 mil and aggregate-reinforced, for a lot's high-traffic bays and its crosswalks. The [system page](/product-book/13) lists the numbers behind it: 8–9 Mohs aggregate, 60 BPN of skid resistance on the ASTM E303 test, and 10+ years of service life. HUB's [parking lots page](/applications/parking-lots) places it at the crosswalks between the stalls and the doors, where it holds under turning traffic.

The Toronto photograph is [page 88](/product-book/88) of the book. [The guide to parking lot markings in preformed thermoplastic](/blog/parking-lot-line-painting-vs-thermoplastic) sets this crosswalk beside the system's other parking lot jobs in Ontario, and covers the arrows and stop bars a lot needs as well.
