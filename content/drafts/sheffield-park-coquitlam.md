On the spread of the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 marked "Across Canada", the only job shown is Sheffield Park in Coquitlam, British Columbia. The headline over it reads "Coast to coast. Setting design standards." The park's paths carry [StreetBond](/products/streetbond) pavement coating from HUB Surface Systems: white dots on a dark path, and curving bands of orange, grey, yellow and white on another.

## The park from the air

The book has two photographs of the park, both taken from above. [Page 83](/product-book/83), captioned "Sheffield Park · Coquitlam, British Columbia", looks straight down on the dotted path where it wraps a curving concrete area. The dots come in mixed sizes, in rows that bend with the path, and a curved concrete band runs through the middle of them. Lawn and a street lined with parked cars fill the top of the frame.

Page 82, facing it, takes a lower and wider view. The dotted path swings past a planted bed set with pale boulders, the banded path runs in from the left, and houses and tall conifers line the slope behind.

## Dots and bands in the coating

StreetBond is a water-based acrylic, modified with epoxy, that goes on asphalt or concrete. On these paths every dot and every band is that coating, laid on the path's own surface, and parks are the first use the book lists for it. The book rates StreetBond at 8+ years of service life and says it is easily refreshed, so worn colour on a path is put right with a new coat.

HUB's page on [parks and paths](/applications/parks-paths) makes the case for a coloured path in its first line: "A well-kept, colourful path invites people into a park." It goes on to say that colour on an existing asphalt path lets the route double as wayfinding, or become a destination in itself.
