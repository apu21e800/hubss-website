For a commercial parking lot, HUB Surface Systems' Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 specifies [TrafficPatternsXD](/products/traffic-patterns-xd) for pedestrian channelization and for the look of pavers, stones and cobbles. Its example is a crosswalk in a grocery store's parking lot in Oakville, Ontario, and the book prints it twice: once labelled "Year 1" and once "Year 10".

## Two photographs on page 73

[Page 73 of the book](/product-book/73) sets the two photographs one above the other, captioned Oakville, Ontario and TrafficPatternsXD. In the Year 1 photograph the red brick units are laid in a herringbone, with a band of grey squares along each edge and a white line outside each band. Two people with a stroller are walking along the front of the store behind it.

In the Year 10 photograph, snow is banked at the edge of the lot and water stands on the asphalt. The herringbone, both grey bands and the white edge lines are all still there to read.

On the facing page, the book's paragraph says that after "10 years of constant use" these crosswalks "have provided a safe and durable path for pedestrians".

## Aggregate through the full 150 mil

TrafficPatternsXD is aggregate-reinforced preformed thermoplastic, 150 mil thick, with aggregate rated 8–9 on the Mohs scale through the full cross-section. The sheets are heated on prepared asphalt and stamped through into the top of it, and the herringbone is the pattern the stamping template pressed in. It goes on asphalt only. The book gives it 60 BPN skid resistance (ASTM E303) and a service life of 10+ years.

Page 86 gives the same system a longer run, 14 years, at the entrance to Toronto Premium Outlets in Halton Hills, Ontario. [The write-up of the Toronto Premium Outlets crosswalks](/blog/toronto-premium-outlets-14-years) covers that job.
