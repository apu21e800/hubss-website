The sky is clear in the photograph, and under it a roundabout in the Cowichan Valley, British Columbia, curves away with an apron of red brick pattern between two concrete curbs. The apron is [StreetPrint stamped asphalt](/products/streetprint), and HUB Surface Systems prints the photograph beside the traffic calming page of its Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5.

## The apron and the road around it

The brick pattern runs the full width of the frame, following the apron round. Beyond it, a yield sign and a pedestrian sign stand at the roadside. The asphalt around the apron is black, and the bank cut for the road is bare earth. Forested hills fill the skyline. The caption names the place, Cowichan Valley, British Columbia.

## Opposite the traffic calming page

The book's [traffic calming spread](/product-book/54) is headed "Drivers slow down with visual cues." Under the headline is the line "A change in surface is a change in speed." Its list of traffic calming devices starts with roundabouts and includes truck aprons, and it specifies StreetPrint for traffic calming device treatments, with StreetBond for the sidewalks, pathways and plazas around them.

HUB's own [traffic calming](/applications/traffic-calming) page names a roundabout apron among the places where colour and texture show a driver the street has changed before a sign does.

## Where the red comes from

The red is [StreetBond](/products/streetbond) pavement coating, laid over a pattern that a steel wire-rope template and a plate compactor have pressed into the heated asphalt. The book's specification for StreetPrint reads "Stamping + StreetBond coating." With the pattern in the asphalt, nothing stands above the road for a plow to catch, and the asphalt stays flexible. The same page specifies new or existing asphalt as the base and gives a service life of 10–20 years.
