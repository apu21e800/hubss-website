The cruise ship terminal walkway in Charlottetown, Prince Edward Island, carries pedestrians and their rolling luggage in the salt air, and HUB Surface Systems' Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 says it was built for all of it. The walkway is [StreetPrint stamped asphalt](/products/streetprint), "stamped in a herringbone basket-weave and sealed with StreetBond," in the book's words.

## Between the cruise berth and the marina

The job has a spread to itself, [pages 134 and 135](/product-book/134), headed "Where the ships come in", and its caption names the Cruise Ship Terminal & Marina Walkway. The spread's one panorama follows the walkway past a cruise ship at the pier and on to the marina, where it curves around a basin of moored boats. At the berth, light standards stand along the pier and a yellow bollard stands near the edge. A rope rail strung between timber posts runs along a yellow timber curb at the water's edge. The pattern reads as small bricks in a dark red-brown, crossed by the shadows of the rope.

## The asphalt under the herringbone

StreetPrint is a pattern stamped into asphalt, then coloured and sealed with [StreetBond](/products/streetbond), as the book describes it. With no pavers laid in it, the walk has nothing to settle or heave, and the book gives StreetPrint a service life of 10–20 years on new or existing asphalt.

The [public spaces page](/applications/public-spaces) makes the same case for plazas and transit forecourts: the look of stone paving, with the asphalt's own flexibility. Each stage of the method is in [how stamped asphalt is installed](/blog/how-stamped-asphalt-is-installed).
