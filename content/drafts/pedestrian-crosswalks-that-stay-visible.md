In White Rock, British Columbia, the city commissioned Vancouver artist Amy (Yun Ru) Bao to design a pedestrian crosswalk that would carry the waterfront inland, and her [Seaside Stroll](/blog/white-rock-langley-trafficpatterns) crosses Johnston Road in the Uptown district. It is a [TrafficPatterns](/products/traffic-patterns) preformed thermoplastic crosswalk, made to the design and heat-applied so it bonds to the asphalt; the book gives it 8+ years of service life. How long a crosswalk stays visible comes down to what it is made of and how it goes in, and the five crossings below show three systems doing that work.

## How the Seaside Stroll was made

White Rock's Uptown had long been overshadowed by the waterfront, and city leaders wanted a landmark that would connect it to the community's identity. Bao's design uses flowing lines that mimic the rhythm of waves and the tones of White Rock's sandy beaches, so the material had to keep the artwork's fine detail without giving anything up on safety. Crews heat-applied preformed TrafficPatterns panels, which captured that detail, and bonded the mural to the asphalt. The Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 carries the crossing on its community branding page.

What keeps it visible is in the sheet. TrafficPatterns is made in the factory to the design at 125 mil, and its anti-skid aggregate is mixed through the whole thickness, so the sheet keeps its grip for as long as it keeps its colour. Its skid resistance in the book is 60 BPN under ASTM E303, and its thermoplastic colours are formulated for UV stability. A TrafficPatterns crossing goes onto asphalt or concrete and is open to traffic within hours.

## Where the Spirit Trail crosses a road

The [Spirit Trail](/blog/spirit-trail-wayfinding-vancouver) on Vancouver's North Shore is planned as a 35 km greenway between Horseshoe Bay in West Vancouver and Deep Cove in the District of North Vancouver, joining up the North Shore's separate public spaces; space2place was the prime consultant for its planning and design. Wherever the trail crosses a principal roadway, the crossing is a [DuraTherm](/products/duratherm) "Spirit" crosswalk, and more have gone in as the trail has grown. Along the way, [DecoMark](/products/decomark) markings work as signage on the pavement, pointing people to the trail and to community amenities.

DuraTherm is inlaid. The asphalt is heated and stamped first, then precut 90 mil thermoplastic is set into the impressions and bonded with an infrared heater, so the finished crosswalk sits flush with the road. The Product Portfolio Book puts the result plainly: "nothing for a plow blade to catch, nothing to trip on." It gives the look of a decorative crosswalk in a surface that has nothing standing above it, and the book's specification lists it as snowplow safe, with no shear risk.

## Look Listen Live at a rail crossing in Brantford

The Children's Safety Village of Brant, in Brantford, Ontario, is a miniature town where local police and fire officers teach children aged 4 to 12 the basics of safety. The village has its own railway crossing, with a gate arm, signal lights and a signal case marked "Donated by CN", and on the sidewalk beside it is a DecoMark marking of the Look Listen Live symbol, the yellow diamond and locomotive of Operation Lifesaver Canada's rail safety campaign. Children on the kindergarten visit practise stopping, looking and listening before they cross the village's roads, and the [write-up of the marking](/blog/look-listen-live-rail-safety-marking-brantford) explains why it sits where it does: it puts the same reminder "where a child is already looking: at the ground, a few steps before the crossing."

DecoMark is HUB's custom graphics system: preformed thermoplastic made in the factory from vector artwork, then heat-applied on site, where it bonds to the surface. In Brantford it is on the concrete sidewalk. Anti-skid aggregate runs through the material, so the grip lasts as the surface wears, and the Product Portfolio Book says the campaign's marking "holds its contrast through freeze-thaw and plow seasons." The same reminder suits a rail crossing near a school or a trail, and since DecoMark is cut from vector artwork, a campaign's symbol is made the same way as a town's crest.

## Crosswalk art on Commercial Drive and in Sechelt

In 2019, three intersections on Vancouver's Commercial Drive got [crosswalks in the green, white and red of the Italian flag](/blog/decorative-crosswalk-commercial-drive), ready for Italian Day on The Drive. They sit at East 1st Avenue, East 4th Avenue and Charles Street, inside the eight blocks the City of Vancouver recognized as its historic Little Italy, and Square One, HUB's master installer in British Columbia, put them in. The crosswalks are TrafficPatterns, and [DecoMark brands the pedestrian areas](/blog/community-spaces) with Little Italy's identity markers. The Product Portfolio Book shows the neighbourhood's own logo set into a corner in DecoMark, fabricated to the business improvement association's artwork and Pantone colour, with a TrafficPatterns crosswalk in the same palette behind it.

In Sechelt, British Columbia, [a pictograph-themed crosswalk at Cowrie Street and Trail Avenue](/blog/pictograph-crosswalk-sechelt) tells the origin story of the shíshálh Nation. It was unveiled by artists Dionne Paul and Lindsey Kyoko Adams, as crosswalk art for the District of Sechelt, and went in during spring 2022 in TrafficPatterns, with materials supplied by Square One Paving. The Product Portfolio Book's creative design page says why that material suits work like this: "Preformed thermoplastic can reproduce artwork while holding the contrast, grip and visibility a crossing is designed for."
