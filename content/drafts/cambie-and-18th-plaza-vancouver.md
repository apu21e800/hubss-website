The plaza on West 18th Avenue, west of Cambie Street, in Vancouver, British Columbia, belongs to the City and is coloured in [StreetBond](/products/streetbond), a pavement coating in the HUB Surface Systems range. The Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 prints it under “Where the neighbourhood starts.”

## Colour across the street and both sidewalks

At the centre of the photograph, a mint ring circles a pale cream disc on the asphalt. Bands of lilac, pink, peach and mint cross the street at angles and carry on over the concrete sidewalks on either side. Orange barriers stand across the far end, and people sit at tables and on a bench along the left-hand sidewalk.

## One coating on the asphalt and the concrete

The book lists StreetBond's surfaces as asphalt and concrete, which is what lets a design like this one carry on from the roadway across the sidewalks. Chemically it is a water-based, epoxy-modified acrylic, and the book offers custom colour matching beyond its standard colours. The service life it prints is 8+ years, and the coating can be refreshed with a new coat when it wears.

## Neighbourhood colour on page 104

[The plaza's own page](/product-book/104) labels it “Neighbourhood colour”, above the headline, and captions it with the city and the coating. StreetBond is one of the systems the book specifies for [public spaces](/applications/public-spaces), on a spread headed “Nobody wants boring.”
