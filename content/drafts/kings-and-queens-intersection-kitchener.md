King Street and Queen Street meet in downtown Kitchener, Ontario, over a work of public art in [TrafficPatterns](/products/traffic-patterns) preformed thermoplastic. The Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 names it "Kings and Queens" and gives it [page 107](/product-book/107).

## Inside the four crosswalks

The book's photograph looks straight down on the intersection. The art fills the middle, with a crosswalk of white bars on each side. A pink circle with a teal crown sits near the centre. Around it are a large sand-coloured circle crossed by wavy bands of yellow, coral and pale blue, purple panels drawn with gold lines, teal wedges and stripes of orange and violet. Red-brick buildings stand on two of the corners.

The page carries a single sentence of text, and it is about the people on foot: the art turns "a historic downtown intersection" into "a pedestrian-friendly space". The caption names the city and the system, and the facing page, 106, is another TrafficPatterns job, the Musqueam Nation land acknowledgement at UBC.

## Thermoplastic at the scale of an intersection

For public art, [the book's page 66](/product-book/66) specifies TrafficPatterns as "large-scale interconnected thermoplastic", beside [DecoMark](/products/decomark) for artwork and graphics and [StreetBond](/products/streetbond) for large-scale colour fields. Here the design covers a whole intersection. TrafficPatterns is made for a design like this in the factory, as sheets cut to the artwork and 125 mil thick, and the sheets are heated on the asphalt until they bond to it.

The system's own page (page 11) gives a return to traffic within hours, and 8+ years as its service life. Skid resistance there is 60 BPN on the ASTM E303 test.
