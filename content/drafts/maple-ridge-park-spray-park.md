An orange bucket hangs from a tall arch at the middle of the Maple Ridge Park spray park, and the pad around it is laid out in deep blue, light blue, orange and grey. The colour is HUB Surface Systems' [StreetBond](/products/streetbond), on a pad in Maple Ridge, British Columbia, that opened in July 2025.

## A full pad on page 108

The photograph on [page 108](/product-book/108) of the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 catches the pad in use. Children run between the spray features and climb onto the boulders set out on it, and adults sit or stretch out on lounge chairs along the edge. The colour lies in large rounded fields that run from the play structure at the far end down to the lawn, and three orange spheres sit toward the front.

Under the photograph the page is labelled Public art, and its headline reads "The playground is the canvas. We do fun." The caption at the foot names the town and the coating: "Maple Ridge, British Columbia | StreetBond".

## Grip comes first

Everyone on a spray pad gets wet and most are barefoot, so the surface has to grip first. The [splash pads](/applications/splash-pads) page sets out the build: StreetBond on the prepared concrete, textured for grip, with pigments that stand up to UV. The book's splash pad spread puts it in four words, "skid resistant, UV stable". The book puts StreetBond's service life at 8+ years, and colour that has worn is restored with a fresh coat.

Where full sun is the worry, the book pairs it on that spread with [StreetBondSR](/products/streetbondsr): StreetBond formulated to reflect, in colours whose solar reflectance is 0.33 or higher, so the surface stays cooler underfoot.

A parks department with a pad on the drawing board can go over StreetBond with HUB at a [Lunch & Learn](/lunch-learn?topic=StreetBond#book). It runs 60 minutes, at your office or by video, and costs the department nothing: lunch is provided, or a $25 voucher if the session is held by video.
