The camera sits almost on the pavement. Red brick units run away from it in staggered courses between two white lines, and snow covers the ground to the left while the road on both sides is wet. This is a [TrafficPatternsXD](/products/traffic-patterns-xd) crosswalk in a new neighbourhood in Kleinburg, Ontario.

HUB Surface Systems gives TrafficPatternsXD [a spread of its own, pages 12 and 13](/product-book/12), in the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5, and the spread opens with this photograph: a full page captioned Kleinburg, Ontario, opposite the system's figures. Behind the crosswalk, new houses with snow on their roofs line the street, and a dark flat-roofed shade structure rises behind a stone wall.

## Brick joints pressed into the asphalt

[The book's installation spread](/product-book/16) takes TrafficPatternsXD in order. The panels are positioned on the prepared asphalt and heated, and then a template is vibrated into the hot material, so it locks into the asphalt below. The joints between the Kleinburg bricks are the lines that template left.

Being stamped into the top of the asphalt is what the site's product page credits for the way the system holds "under plow blades, road salt and the freeze-thaw that follows". It is an asphalt system, and concrete is outside its range.

## The figures on page 13

The page facing the photograph is the system's own. Under its headline the book prints "Aggregate-reinforced, preformed thermoplastic. Traffic tough, proven coast to coast." The figures on the page are a thickness of 150 mil, aggregate rated 8–9 on the Mohs scale, skid resistance of 60 BPN under ASTM E303, and a service life of 10+ years.
