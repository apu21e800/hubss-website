Every couple of years the driveway goes grey again, and a weekend goes to driveway sealing, with the cars on the street until it dries. It keeps the surface looking cared for.

A coat of sealer brings back the black and slows the sun and water down. It wears off first where the tires turn, which is why the job comes round again. Two other answers sit beside it, and the asphalt underneath decides between all three.

## A maintenance coating, for asphalt that is sound

The Product Portfolio Book calls [DuraShield](/products/durashield) a pavement maintenance coating and lists driveways among its uses. The book describes a coating that resists fuel, oil and de-icing agents, cools the surface, and protects the pavement underneath, extending its service life. Its specification line is shorter still: preserves and protects asphalt, old and new. It is a two-component epoxy-modified acrylic, and its solar-reflective option, SR Gray, is 0.35 on the 2026 colour card. The book's townhome pages specify the same coating for shared-drive maintenance.

HUB's Residential Driveways page says when it fits: where an older driveway is sound but tired, DuraShield protects it and extends its service life. Sound, in plain words, means the trouble is on top. The colour has gone and the surface is wearing, but the driveway is flat, it drains, and nothing is sinking or breaking up.

## When stamped asphalt is the better answer

The Product Portfolio Book opens its projects section on the laneways of Oxford Townhomes in New Westminster, British Columbia: charcoal [StreetPrint](/products/streetprint) on a strata's shared drives. Its driveway page starts from a line a homeowner will recognize: most driveways fail on appearance long before they fail on structure.

StreetPrint changes the appearance and works with the asphalt already there. Infrared heaters bring it back to a workable temperature, a steel wire-rope template presses a cobblestone, brick, herringbone or slate pattern into it, and StreetBond pavement coating colours it, with no removal, no repaving and no excavation. The surface stays flush, so there is nothing for a plow blade to catch, no joints to weed and no pavers to settle, and the asphalt stays flexible. The book gives StreetPrint a service life of 10–20 years, and years on, the colour is renewed with a fresh coat. [HUB's installation guide](/blog/how-stamped-asphalt-is-installed) walks through the steps.

So it is the better answer when the driveway is sound and you want it to look like brick or stone rather than black again. The installation guide states the condition plainly: sound pavement takes a pattern, and failing pavement needs to be addressed first. It is also an answer when the driveway is due to be repaved anyway, since the book's base line for StreetPrint is new or existing asphalt. Two earlier guides go further, on [stamped asphalt beside interlocking stone](/blog/residential-driveways-stamped-asphalt) and on [the upgrade from a plain driveway](/blog/residential-driveways-stamped-asphalt-upgrade).

## Driveway sealing, by what the driveway shows

| What the driveway shows | What it calls for |
| --- | --- |
| Grey and worn on top, but flat, whole and draining | A reseal, or a DuraShield maintenance coating |
| Sound, and you want it to look like brick or stone | StreetPrint, stamped into the asphalt already there |
| Sinking, breaking up, or due to be repaved | The base first, and StreetPrint can go into the new asphalt |
