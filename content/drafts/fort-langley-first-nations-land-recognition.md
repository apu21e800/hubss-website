A First Nations land recognition lies on the asphalt in Fort Langley, British Columbia: a sand-coloured circle holding four rounded forms in green, yellow, blue and red around a white disc with a four-pointed star. It is [DecoMark](/products/decomark), fabricated to the design's artwork in HUB Surface Systems' colour palette, and page 114 of the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 is given over to it.

## The words on page 114

On [page 114](/product-book/114) the book heads the job "First Nations Land Recognition." and sets a land acknowledgement beneath the heading, in quotation marks. It begins: "We acknowledge those lands that are the traditional, ancestral, and unceded territories of Canada's First Nations, Inuit, and Métis peoples." It ends with a commitment "to walking forward together in the spirit of truth and reconciliation."

The page gives no name beside the words. It sits among the book's community branding projects, and its last line gives the town and the system: "Fort Langley, British Columbia | DecoMark".

## A circle on the asphalt

The full photograph looks across the circle to a broad flight of steps that climbs between grass terraces. Each of the four forms carries white crescents and a white three-armed shape, and the red one, nearest the camera, is the largest. Around the circle the asphalt is plain and dark.

## Heat-applied to the asphalt

DecoMark starts as factory-made sheets of preformed thermoplastic, cut to the design's vector artwork in standard and premium colours. On the job the pieces are set out where the design goes, then heated, and they bond to the pavement beneath. The book's creative design spread gives the material as 125 mil, about 3 mm. The anti-skid aggregate, a hard grit, runs through the sheet itself, so it still grips as it wears down, and the pavement below is left uncut. Identity is the first of the uses its page in the book lists, before wayfinding, public art and memorial.

The site's [community branding](/applications/community-branding) page names DecoMark as the system for work like this, and the book's community branding spread gives its job in a few words: "horizontal wayfinding and community/BIA branding".
