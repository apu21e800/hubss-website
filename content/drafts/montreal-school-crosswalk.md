At an intersection in Montréal, Québec, as HUB Surface Systems' Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 captions it, every crosswalk is a band of small coloured squares. The caption names [TrafficPatterns](/products/traffic-patterns), and the page's text pairs it with [StreetBond](/products/streetbond), "a combination of TrafficPatterns and StreetBond." The page's headline is one plain sentence: "School crosswalks should be seen."

## Colour on all four sides

The bands run around all four sides of the intersection, in squares of purple, blue, green, yellow, orange and red, edged in black and white. The asphalt is wet and shining. In the foreground the sidewalk corner is coloured too, in large triangles of purple, pink, blue and green.

## Two systems at one intersection

TrafficPatterns arrives as 125 mil preformed thermoplastic, cut in the factory to the design, and on site it is heated and bonds to the asphalt or concrete beneath. Because its anti-skid aggregate is mixed all the way through the sheet, wear exposes more of it, and the grip keeps pace with the colour. Its own page in the book, [page 11](/product-book/11), sets the numbers: back in use within hours, 60 BPN (ASTM E303) for skid resistance, and 8+ years of service life.

StreetBond's page in the book is headed "The colour." It is a pavement coating for asphalt or concrete, with a service life of 8+ years. The book pairs the two here without saying which surface carries which.

## Seen at driving speed

The page sits in the book under "Traffic calming", and its text says the colour is there for visibility and to "create awareness in the community." HUB's [pedestrian safety page](/applications/pedestrian-safety) sets the test a school zone treatment has to pass: it "has to read at driving speed." The [crosswalks page](/applications/crosswalks) names the two jobs any crosswalk has: it tells the driver someone may cross, and it keeps its grip underfoot.

The photograph and its headline are on [page 127](/product-book/127) of the book.
