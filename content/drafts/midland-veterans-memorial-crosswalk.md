Members of Royal Canadian Legion Branch 80 unveiled Midland's Veterans Memorial Crosswalk on November 6, 2026, five days before Remembrance Day. It crosses at King Street and Ellen Street in Midland, Ontario, beside the Midland Cenotaph, where the Legion holds its Remembrance Day ceremony. The crosswalk is [TrafficPatterns](/products/traffic-patterns) preformed thermoplastic from HUB Surface Systems.

## Maple leaves and one line in two languages

The Legion provided the design, the Town says. Red maple leaves run down the middle of the crosswalk, over bars of grey and white inside a white border. At one end a line in black reads "In honour of those who served and sacrificed." At the other end the same dedication is in French: "En hommage à ceux et celles qui ont servi et se sont sacrifiés."

Seen from the French end, the cenotaph stands in the park behind the crosswalk, so the dedication and the monument share one view.

## Heat-applied on the road

The artwork goes into the material at the factory. TrafficPatterns is preformed thermoplastic made to the design, 125 mil thick, and on site the finished pieces are heat-applied to the road, where they bond to its surface. The Town's photos of the installation show a heater passing over a maple leaf, with the grey and white bars already laid around it.

The anti-skid grit is mixed right through the material, so the crosswalk keeps its grip as it wears. The Product Portfolio Book's TrafficPatterns page ([page 11](/product-book/11)) lists a skid resistance of 60 BPN, measured to ASTM E303, and a service life of 8+ years.

## How it was received

After the unveiling, the Town's operations staff wrote to HUB that the crosswalk was received very well, with compliments from the Legion, the Mayor and Council. HUB had worked with the Town's staff on the product before it went down. The Town's own account of the day, with a photograph of pipers and veterans crossing, is [on midland.ca](https://www.midland.ca/news-notices/posts/midland-unveils-veterans-memorial-crosswalk-and-shares-remembrance-day-service-updates/).

A memorial crosswalk is still a crosswalk. It meets the same plows and the same salt as the one at the next corner, so it is specified the same way, by its grip and its service life. Kitchener's veterans crosswalk, also in TrafficPatterns, is [written up here](/blog/veterans-crosswalk-kitchener).
