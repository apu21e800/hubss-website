From above, the photograph shows the path through the courtyard at Green Square curving between two lawns in blocks and bands of navy, pale blue, orange, yellow, olive green and sand. The colour is [StreetBond](/products/streetbond) pavement coating. Green Square is in Kelowna, British Columbia, in the city's Lower Mission.

## Games laid out on the path

White lines mark a basketball key beside a hoop at the edge of the path, and lines numbered 15 and 20 cross the coloured blocks. At the far end are two tic-tac-toe grids and a hopscotch. Past them are a playground, raised garden beds in round metal planters and the balconies of the buildings around the courtyard.

## A coating that moves with the pavement

StreetBond comes in two parts and is water-based: an acrylic modified with epoxy, for asphalt or concrete. [Page 25 of the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5](/product-book/25) describes it as made to flex as the pavement under it moves, and gives its service life as 8+ years, easily refreshed. The range runs to 63 standard colours, with full custom matching beyond them. Along the foot of the same page the book prints StreetBond's uses: parks, playgrounds, courts and area markings. In Kelowna, the basketball key and the games share one coated path.

## Colour for a development's courtyard

The book's [townhomes](/applications/townhomes) page (page 74) is written for townhome and strata developments; it names StreetBond for colour and sealing, with [StreetPrint stamped asphalt](/products/streetprint) and [DuraShield](/products/durashield) beside it. HUB Surface Systems' own townhomes page notes that in a townhome or strata development the owners who live there pay for the shared surfaces, so upkeep counts as much as looks.
