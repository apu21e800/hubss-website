"Lit by starlight" is the headline HUB Surface Systems' Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 gives the summit terrace at Mont-Mégantic, Québec. The terrace lies between the observatory's domes, and its surface is white [StreetBondSR](/products/streetbondsr).

## Where outdoor lighting is kept to a minimum

The page explains the colour in two sentences: "Inside the world's first International Dark Sky Reserve, outdoor lighting is kept to a bare minimum. A white StreetBondSR surface reflects what little light there is, so the summit's paths and plazas can be seen by starlight."

## The terrace by day

The photograph on [page 102](/product-book/102) was taken under a blue sky. The white runs right up to the metal-clad base of an observatory dome, with a geodesic dome beyond it and evergreens along the edge of the summit. A second photograph, from further back, takes in more of the terrace: a building with a curved roof and a roll-up door, and a railing with a wooden top rail along its edge.

## The same coating against the sun

Everywhere else in the book, StreetBondSR is about heat. Its own page, which pictures a fleet lot in Toronto, is headed "Solar reflective coating", and the park path in Osoyoos, British Columbia, on pages 100 and 101 is printed as running "cooler underfoot" three years on. The system is StreetBond in colours made to reflect the sun's energy instead of absorbing it, with initial solar reflectance at 0.33 or higher, a level the book says can contribute to LEED v5 heat island credits on surfaces other than roofs. The book prints its service life as 8+ years and its surface as asphalt. Plazas are among the uses on its page, and on Mont-Mégantic the plaza is the terrace between the domes.
