# The product is half of it

Every specification for a decorative surface contains an assumption that rarely gets written down: that somebody within driving distance knows how to install it.

For preformed thermoplastic that assumption is doing a lot of work. The material is heat-applied — it is fused into the asphalt, not glued to it — and the difference between a crossing that lasts a decade and one that lifts at the edges in year two is almost entirely in the application. Same for stamped asphalt, where the reheat temperature and the compaction after stamping decide whether the pattern holds.

This is why HUB runs a certified installer program rather than simply selling material.

## What "certified" means here

Certified installers are trained and authorized by HUB to install each system to specification. They are not general paving contractors who have bought a pallet; they are crews who have been taken through the systems they install, by the people who supply them.

For a procurement team, that credential is doing something specific: it means the performance figures in the spec — the service life, the skid resistance, the bond — were achieved by an installation method the manufacturer recognises.

Behind them, HUB runs two regional offices: **East** in Milton, Ontario, and **West** in Ladysmith, British Columbia. Two offices, one network, one country.

## What this means for a specification

Three practical consequences worth knowing when you are writing the document:

**1. You can name the standard, not just the product.** "Installed by a HUB-certified applicator to manufacturer specification" is a line that does real work in a tender, because it makes the installation method part of what is being bid.

**2. Availability is a real constraint, and it is knowable in advance.** Before a project goes to tender it is worth asking who is certified in your province and what their season looks like. That is a phone call, and it is better made in February than in August.

**3. The supplier stays in the conversation.** With a manufacturer-backed network, technical support during installation is not a favour — it is how the program is designed to work.
