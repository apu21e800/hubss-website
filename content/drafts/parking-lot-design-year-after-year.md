After fourteen Ontario winters, the [TrafficPatternsXD](/products/traffic-patterns-xd) crosswalks at the entrance to Toronto Premium Outlets in Halton Hills, Ontario, still carry shoppers and delivery trucks. Page 86 of the Product Portfolio Book · Volume 5 sums the job up in four words: "Still performing every day." For parking lot design at a commercial property, that is the evidence worth having: one surface, one property, and years of the traffic a real lot carries.

## Fourteen years at the Toronto Premium Outlets entrance

HUB Surface Systems' [write-up of the job](/blog/toronto-premium-outlets-14-years) starts from what a retail lot does to a surface. A retail lot, it says, combines "the things that destroy surface markings (concentrated turning movements, delivery vehicles, salt, plows) with the one thing a municipal road does not have: an owner whose customers judge the property on how it looks."

The crosswalks are aggregate-reinforced preformed thermoplastic, 150 mil thick, heated on the prepared asphalt and stamped through into the top of it; TrafficPatternsXD goes on asphalt only. The pattern reads as laid masonry, so a driver sees different ground where people cross. The aggregate is rated 8–9 on the Mohs scale and runs through the full cross-section, so, as the write-up notes, the surface has not gone slick where the wheel paths cross it. The Product Portfolio Book gives TrafficPatternsXD a skid resistance of 60 BPN (ASTM E303) and a service life of 10+ years.

The book's Commercial spaces spread says it again: "TrafficPatternsXD performs year after year." On [page 73](/product-book/73) the same grocery-store crosswalk in Oakville, Ontario, appears twice, new and ten years on, over the line "After 10 years of constant use, these crossings have provided a safe and durable path for pedestrians."

## Where customers walk first

The book's page on Granville Island in Vancouver, British Columbia, is headed "The first thing a customer walks on." The decorative crosswalk outside Granville Island Brewing is TrafficPatternsXD, and the book prints it as seven years old. In its photograph a concrete mixer truck stands at the crosswalk's edge, and bicycles lean by the taproom door beyond it.

HUB's [page on commercial spaces](/applications/commercial-spaces) makes the same point for any retail centre or hotel entrance: customers and tenants read the property before they walk in, and the surface at the door is the first thing underfoot.

At the Audi dealership in Vaughan, Ontario, TrafficPatternsXD marks the arrival. The book's words for it begin "Like the Audi brand, crisp, clean lines" and end "Accenting the building in a unique way." In its photograph the pattern is a grid of pale grey squares edged with a darker band, laid up to the grey cladding of the building. It is the same 150 mil material as the red brick at Halton Hills, in a pattern and colour chosen for the brand. The book prints the thermoplastic colour range, and physical samples are available by request.

## Stamped asphalt on a strata's laneways

A townhome or strata development is judged at the curb, and its shared surfaces are paid for by the owners who live there, so upkeep matters as much as looks. At Oxford Townhomes in Queensborough, New Westminster, British Columbia, the laneways are charcoal [StreetPrint stamped asphalt](/products/streetprint). The Product Portfolio Book opens its Projects section with them: a dark stone pattern, wet in the photograph, running between rows of grey townhomes.

StreetPrint is the original stamped asphalt, a Canadian invention installed since 1992. A steel wire-rope template presses the pattern into new or existing asphalt, and [StreetBond](/products/streetbond) pavement coating colours it. The finished surface stays flush, and the book spells out what follows: nothing for a plow blade to catch, no joints to weed, no pavers to settle, and asphalt that stays flexible. Its service life is 10–20 years, and when the colour wears, a crew recoats it.

The book's Townhomes spread describes "entry courts and shared drives with the look of clay pavers or stone cobble", and specifies StreetPrint for stamped entry courts and driveways with StreetBond for colour and sealing. The work on site is set out in [how stamped asphalt is installed](/blog/how-stamped-asphalt-is-installed), and HUB's [townhome and strata page](/applications/townhomes) has more of this work.

## PreMark on a hotel access road in Delta

At Cascades Casino Delta, in Delta, British Columbia, a ladder crosswalk in [PreMark](/products/premark) crosses the access road to the new hotel. It is the photograph on PreMark's page in the Product Portfolio Book: white bars between two edge lines on fresh black asphalt, with the hotel still under construction behind.

PreMark is preformed thermoplastic for regulatory markings: arrows, stop bars, yield triangles, school legends, bike symbols and ladder lines, pre-cut to specification and heat-applied by torch until they bond to the surface. With no stencils and no curing window, the lot can be driven on as soon as the crew moves on. The glass beads in the material send a headlight back to the driver at night. It is 125 mil, or 90 mil in the ViziGrip option, and the book gives it a service life of 6–8 years.

That is where it differs from the decorative sheets. TrafficPatternsXD carries anti-skid aggregate but no glass beads, so the lines and symbols a driver has to read at night in the rain go down in PreMark, and the pattern people walk on goes down in TrafficPatternsXD. [The guide to parking lot markings](/blog/parking-lot-line-painting-vs-thermoplastic) goes through which of a lot's markings to put in preformed thermoplastic.

## Matching parking lot design to the wear

The Product Portfolio Book's Parking lots spread puts it plainly: "Parking lots need to be tough." It specifies TrafficPatternsXD for "high-traffic bays and crossings" and StreetPrint as "stamped asphalt treated with StreetBond coating". The jobs above show where each one has gone.

| Where the wear is | System | How it goes in | Service life | On record |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Crosswalks and entrances under turning traffic and trucks | TrafficPatternsXD, 150 mil, aggregate-reinforced | Heated and stamped into the asphalt; asphalt only | 10+ years | Toronto Premium Outlets, fourteen years; Granville Island, seven years; Audi, Vaughan |
| Entry courts, laneways and drives that should read as stone | StreetPrint stamped asphalt | Pattern stamped into new or existing asphalt, coloured with StreetBond | 10–20 years | Oxford Townhomes, New Westminster |
| Arrows, stop bars, symbols and crosswalk lines | PreMark, 125 mil, glass beads | Heat-applied by torch, bonds to the surface; drive on immediately | 6–8 years | Cascades Casino Delta access road |

Where the wear is wheels turning on a crosswalk, the aggregate decides it, and TrafficPatternsXD is, in the book's words, the toughest material in the product range. It needs asphalt, though. A crosswalk on concrete takes [TrafficPatterns](/products/traffic-patterns), the 125 mil sheet that is heat-applied and bonds to concrete as well as asphalt, with a service life of 8+ years.

Where the whole surface should read as stone and the asphalt is new or sound, StreetPrint puts the pattern in the asphalt itself, so there are no units to settle. Where a marking is an instruction to a driver rather than decorative pavement, it is PreMark.
