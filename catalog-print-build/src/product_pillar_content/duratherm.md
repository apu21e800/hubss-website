WHAT IT IS
An inlaid preformed thermoplastic pavement marking system. A specialized infrared heater softens the asphalt, a template imprints depressions into the heated surface, and precut DuraTherm sheets are placed into the depressions, then thermally bonded.

The thermoplastic sits SLIGHTLY BELOW the surface level of the surrounding asphalt — 'snowplow friendly.' This sub-flush profile is what makes DuraTherm uniquely tolerant of snowplow blades and aggressive winter maintenance.

KEY FEATURES
- Sub-flush installation — thermoplastic sits slightly below the surrounding asphalt for plow tolerance.
- Imprinted, not milled — depression formed by template + heat + vibratory plate compactor (700–900 lb).
- 90 mil minimum thickness.
- Heat-fused to asphalt by Ennis-Flint StreetHeat® / Magnum / Flint2000EX equipment.
- Anti-skid: minimum 6 Mohs intermix, minimum 8 Mohs surface-applied.
- Manufactured without glass beads — DuraTherm is non-reflective.
- ISO 9001 certified manufacturing; ADA-compliant.
- Must be installed by an Ennis-Flint TrafficScapes Certified Applicator. HUBSS is the Canadian certifying body.

TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS
- Material: Preformed thermoplastic — ester-modified rosin, aggregates, pigments, binders, anti-skid elements
- Minimum thickness: 90 mil (2.3 mm)
- Sheet sizes: Primary pattern sheets 24×24 in. Borders 8 or 12 in wide × 24 in long.
- Template sizes: 150-mil thickness; standard crosswalk widths 6 ft to 20 ft in 2-ft increments
- Anti-skid (intermixed): Min 30% intermix, min 6 Mohs hardness
- Anti-skid (surface): Min 8 Mohs hardness
- Skid resistance: Min 60 BPN per ASTM E303
- Static friction: Min 0.6 (wet & dry) per ASTM C1028 / ASTM D2047
- Minimum install temperature: 40°F (5°C) ambient and road — DO NOT apply below 5°C
- Substrate: ASPHALT ONLY — specification explicitly excludes Portland cement concrete
- Installation: Template + 700–900 lb vibratory plate compactor + Ennis-Flint infrared heating equipment
- Storage: 12 months indoors

APPLICATIONS
- Public roadway crosswalks (typically inlaid between standard white crosswalk lines)
- Streetscape elements and traffic-calming patterns
- Heritage districts and community-branding inlays
- Residential and commercial pedestrian/vehicular areas
- Pedestrian plazas with vehicular access through the area
- Anywhere snowplow tolerance is the deciding factor

CORRECTIONS FROM CURRENT SITE COPY (Doug to confirm)

- Site says "embedded into a milled groove" — actually template-imprinted into heated asphalt (NOT cold milling)
